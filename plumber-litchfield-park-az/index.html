City Overview

A Small City With Its Own Water Utility

Coverage

Daily technician routes cover Litchfield Park, from the historic Old Litchfield core to the golf-adjacent communities surrounding the Wigwam Resort.

Response

Litchfield Park is one of the few cities we serve that isn't on a municipal water department at all. Water here comes from Liberty Utilities, a private company, rather than a city-run water resources department like Buckeye, Goodyear, or Avondale. That distinction matters more than it sounds. In 2023, Litchfield Park and parts of northern Goodyear went through a well-documented water pressure crisis that drew Arizona Corporation Commission involvement, with some residents losing landscaping and one retirement community even facing move-in delays over pressure that couldn't support normal household use.

Litchfield Park's history is also genuinely different from its neighbors. The city grew out of a company town Goodyear Tire established in the 1910s, and its historic core, Old Litchfield, is built largely from 1950s and 1960s ranch homes, decades older than the master-planned norm across most of this network's coverage area.

That combination, a private utility with a real pressure history and a genuinely older housing stock, means diagnosing a Litchfield Park plumbing problem starts from a different set of assumptions than a call in one of Buckeye's newer subdivisions.

Local Plumbing Challenges

What Litchfield Park's Water And Age Do To Plumbing

Very Hard Liberty Utilities Water

Liberty Utilities delivers water to Litchfield Park in the range of 12 to 18 grains per gallon depending on the specific source blend, putting it solidly in the "very hard" category and accelerating scale buildup in water heaters and fixtures across the city.

A Real, Documented Pressure History

Unlike a generic "check your pressure" warning, Litchfield Park has an actual, reported history of utility-side pressure problems significant enough to draw state regulatory attention. If you're noticing weak pressure, it's worth ruling out a household-side cause before assuming it's isolated to your plumbing.

Old Litchfield's Original Plumbing

Ranch homes from the 1950s and 1960s in the historic core may still have original or early-replacement plumbing, including cast iron or clay sewer lines that are now well past a typical service life.

Mature Tree Root Intrusion

Decades-old landscaping in Old Litchfield means real root intrusion risk into older clay and cast iron sewer lines, a factor that barely exists in this network's newer master-planned coverage areas.
