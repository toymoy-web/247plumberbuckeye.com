Chlorine Taste or Hard Water? How to Tell What's Actually Wrong With Your Buckeye Tap Water

By the Buckeye 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-10

Softeners and filtration systems get bundled together in marketing so often that a lot of homeowners assume they're the same fix for the same problem. They're not, and knowing which complaint you actually have matters before spending money on the wrong solution.

The Hardness Complaint

Hardness shows up as physical effects: chalky white residue on dishes and glassware, soap that won't lather well, faster-than-expected scale buildup on faucets and showerheads, and dry, itchy skin after a shower. It's a mineral problem, specifically calcium and magnesium, and it's a softener's job to address it.

Buckeye's groundwater runs consistently hard across the entire city, so if you're noticing these physical effects, hardness is very likely the cause, not something specific to your particular plumbing.

The Taste and Odor Complaint

Taste and odor complaints are a different category entirely: a chlorine smell or taste, a slightly metallic or earthy flavor, or water that just doesn't taste as clean as you'd expect. None of these are caused by hardness. Chlorine taste comes from municipal disinfection treatment, and it's a filtration problem, not a softening one.

Can You Have Both?

Yes, and it's actually common. Hardness and chlorine taste come from entirely different sources, so a household can genuinely have both complaints at once without either one causing the other. If that's your situation, a softener and a filtration system address two separate problems and typically get installed together rather than one covering both.

A Quick Way To Tell Them Apart

If the complaint is about how the water feels, on skin, on dishes, or on fixtures, that's a hardness question. If the complaint is about how the water tastes or smells, that's a filtration question. It's a simple distinction, but it's the one that determines which system actually solves your specific problem.

What Happens If You Guess Wrong

Installing a softener when the real complaint is chlorine taste won't fix the taste at all, since a softener isn't designed to filter chlorine or improve flavor. Installing a filtration system when the real complaint is hardness won't stop scale buildup, since filtration doesn't remove calcium and magnesium the way a softener does. Getting the diagnosis right the first time saves the cost of realizing later that the wrong system was installed.

Getting An Honest Recommendation

A free on-site assessment can confirm which complaint actually applies to your water and household, rather than guessing based on what a neighbor installed or what a general online article suggests. Every Buckeye, Goodyear, and Avondale address has slightly different water conditions depending on the specific supply blend reaching that neighborhood, so a real assessment beats a generic assumption every time.

Why Marketing Blurs The Two Problems

A lot of water treatment marketing bundles softening and filtration language together under phrases like "water quality system" or "whole-home water solution," which makes it genuinely hard to tell from an ad alone which specific problem a product actually solves. Reading past the marketing language to the actual mechanism, ion exchange for hardness versus carbon filtration for taste and chlorine, is the more reliable way to match a purchase to your actual complaint.

What A Combined System Looks Like

For households dealing with both hardness and taste complaints, a combined setup typically means a softener installed first in the water line, followed by a filtration stage, so the water gets treated for minerals and then for taste and odor in sequence. This is more common than people expect once they understand the two problems are separate, since Buckeye's water genuinely can carry both a hardness issue and a taste issue at the same time.

A Simple Test You Can Do At Home

If you're still not sure which complaint applies, run a glass of cold tap water and let it sit for a minute. A chlorine smell that's noticeable right away points to a taste and odor issue. Separately, check for chalky residue on a recently washed glass or plate held up to the light; visible white film points to hardness. It's not a lab-grade test, but it's a reasonable starting point before an on-site assessment.

What About Bottled Water Or Pitcher Filters In The Meantime?

If you're dealing with a taste complaint while deciding on a permanent fix, a pitcher filter or bottled water can address drinking water in the short term, but neither treats the water used for bathing, laundry, or dishes, where both hardness and chlorine still show up. They're a reasonable stopgap, not a long-term solution to either complaint.

How Long Installation Actually Takes

A whole-home softener or filtration system installation is typically completed in a single visit, often just a few hours depending on your home's existing plumbing layout and whether any additional piping is needed to route water through the new equipment. Neither system requires extensive remodeling or downtime once the assessment has determined the right setup for your home.

Maintenance Once A System Is Installed

A softener needs periodic salt refills and, less often, resin bed maintenance over years of use. A filtration system needs filter cartridge changes on a schedule that depends on your household's water usage and the specific filter media installed. Neither requires constant attention, but both perform best with a routine maintenance schedule rather than being ignored until performance visibly drops.

Where To Go From Here

If you've identified which complaint applies to your household, the next step is a free on-site assessment to confirm the specific fix and get a real price, rather than an estimate based on general assumptions about your water. If you're still not sure, describe what you're noticing when you call, and we'll help narrow it down before recommending anything.
