Slab Leak Warning Signs Every Buckeye Homeowner Should Know

By the Buckeye 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-10

A slab leak is one of the easiest plumbing problems to miss, because it happens underneath your floor rather than somewhere you can see it. In Buckeye, where almost every home built in the last two decades sits on a concrete slab rather than a basement or crawlspace, that makes slab leaks a real and fairly common call for us. Here's what actually tips people off before the damage gets serious.

Most of what follows comes from patterns we see repeatedly on service calls, not a generic checklist. None of these signs guarantee a slab leak by themselves, but they're the specific things worth noticing before a small leak becomes an expensive one to fix.

A Warm Spot on the Floor

This is the single most common way people first notice a slab leak. If the hot water line under your slab has developed a leak, the water tracking through the concrete can warm a specific patch of flooring, sometimes only a foot or two across. It's most noticeable on tile or bare concrete, since carpet and other flooring insulate the effect somewhat.

A cold spot can point to the same problem on a supply line before it reaches the water heater, though this is less common than the warm-spot version. Either way, an isolated temperature difference in one part of an otherwise consistent floor is worth investigating rather than dismissing as normal variation.

A Water Bill That Doesn't Add Up

If your household's water usage hasn't changed but your bill has climbed, a slab leak is one of the more common explanations, especially if you've also ruled out an obvious source like a running toilet or an irrigation line. A leak under the slab can run for weeks before it's visible anywhere else, and it's still being metered the whole time.

It's worth comparing at least two or three recent bills rather than one unusual month, since a single spike can have other explanations, like a house full of guests or an unusually long irrigation cycle. A steady upward trend with no obvious household reason is the pattern that points more specifically to a leak.

Running Water You Can't Explain

Turn off every fixture in the house and listen. If you can still hear water running, or your water meter's flow indicator is still spinning, water is moving somewhere in the system that shouldn't be. That's one of the more reliable manual checks before calling anyone out.

Low Pressure at One or More Fixtures

A slab leak reduces pressure to whatever's downstream of it on the same line, so a sudden, isolated pressure drop at a specific fixture, rather than the whole house, is worth paying attention to.

Why It's Worth Acting on Early

None of these signs guarantee a slab leak on their own, but two or more together are worth a proper look. Electronic leak detection can pinpoint the leak's location before any concrete is opened, which keeps the repair to a small, targeted access point instead of an exploratory search. The earlier it's caught, the less water is lost and the smaller the area affected.

Waiting rarely makes the situation better. A slab leak doesn't heal itself, and the longer it runs, the more likely it is to affect a wider area of flooring or, in some cases, contribute to moisture problems in nearby walls or baseboards. The cost of an early, targeted repair is consistently smaller than the cost of a leak that's been running for months, and it's rarely worth gambling on a problem resolving on its own.

If you're noticing any of these signs in a Buckeye, Goodyear, or Avondale home, it's worth a call rather than waiting to see if it gets worse. Slab leaks generally don't fix themselves, and the water usage adds up every day they run, both in wasted water and in the risk of the affected area growing before a technician ever takes a proper look at it.

What Happens During Electronic Leak Detection

Electronic leak detection doesn't involve breaking up your floor to look for a problem. A technician isolates sections of the plumbing system and uses acoustic and electronic tracing equipment to listen for the leak's specific signature, narrowing its location to a small target area rather than an entire room. That precision is what keeps the eventual repair to a single, targeted access point.

Once the leak is located, the repair itself depends on the pipe material and how it's accessed. Copper and PEX lines are repaired differently, and a leak under a tiled area costs more to access than one under an open garage slab. Either way, you get a specific location and a specific price before anything is opened up.

What To Do While You Wait For A Technician

If you suspect a slab leak, shutting off the water at the main isn't always necessary, but it's worth doing if the leak seems to be actively worsening or if you're leaving the house for an extended period. Avoid running hot water unnecessarily if you suspect the leak is on the hot line, since that just adds to the volume moving through the damaged section.

It's also worth taking a photo of your water meter reading and noting the time, then checking it again after an hour with everything in the house turned off. A meter that's still moving confirms water is escaping somewhere, which is useful information to share when you call, and it can help narrow down whether the issue is on the hot side, the cold side, or somewhere in between.
