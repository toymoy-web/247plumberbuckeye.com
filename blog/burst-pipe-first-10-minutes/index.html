What To Do In The First 10 Minutes of a Burst Pipe

By the Buckeye 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-10

A burst pipe is one of the few plumbing emergencies where the first few minutes genuinely change the outcome. Water loss and property damage compound by the minute, so the priority isn't diagnosis, it's stopping the water and limiting the damage until help arrives.

This isn't a list of things that might help. It's the specific, ordered sequence worth knowing before you ever need it, since trying to figure this out for the first time during an actual emergency wastes exactly the minutes that matter most.

Read through this once now, while nothing is actually wrong. That's genuinely the best time to learn it, since the version of you dealing with an actual burst pipe won't have the calm attention to read carefully.

1. Shut Off The Main Water Supply

Find your home's main shutoff valve, usually near the water meter or where the main line enters the house, and turn it off completely. This stops the water at the source rather than just at the fixture closest to the break, which matters if the burst is inside a wall or under the floor where you can't see or reach it directly.

If you don't know where your main shutoff is, this is worth finding today, not during an emergency. It's typically a lever or wheel valve near the street-facing side of the house or in a utility closet.

2. Turn Off Electricity To The Affected Area If Water Is Near Outlets Or Fixtures

If water is pooling near electrical outlets, switches, or fixtures, and you can safely reach your home's breaker panel without walking through standing water, shut off power to that area. If reaching the panel means walking through water, don't. Wait for help rather than risking electric shock.

3. Contain What You Can, Safely

Towels, buckets, and moving furniture or belongings out of the affected area can reduce secondary damage while you wait for a technician. This is worth doing only if it doesn't put you in the path of actively flowing water or require reaching into an area where the pipe is still failing.

4. Call For Emergency Service

Once the water is off, call for emergency plumbing service. Describe what happened, whether the water is still off, and roughly how much water has already gotten into the space. That information helps us prioritize dispatch and bring the right equipment on the first visit, rather than showing up and having to figure it out from scratch.

5. Document The Damage Before Cleanup Begins

If your homeowner's insurance is likely to be involved, a few photos of the affected area before extensive cleanup starts can matter for your claim. This doesn't need to delay getting water off the floor; a quick round of photos takes under a minute.

What Not To Do

Don't use any electrical appliances in or near standing water. Don't attempt to repair the pipe yourself while water is still under pressure. Don't wait to see if it stops on its own; a burst pipe doesn't self-resolve, and every additional minute adds to both the water loss and the potential damage.

After The Water Is Off

Once the immediate emergency is contained, the next steps are repairing the actual pipe failure and assessing whether the water has caused damage beyond the immediate area that needs separate restoration work. Both are worth handling promptly rather than living with a temporary patch while damage continues to set in unseen.

Why This Happens More Than People Expect In Buckeye

Buckeye's hard groundwater accelerates internal pipe corrosion over years, which is a real, distinct factor behind burst pipes here compared to a city with softer water. A pipe that thins from the inside due to mineral exposure can go from a slow, undetected weak spot to a sudden full failure with very little warning, which is part of why this happens more often than homeowners moving from other regions might expect.

A Few Things Worth Doing Before You Ever Need This

Locate your main shutoff valve today and make sure everyone in the household knows where it is, not just one person. Keep our number saved somewhere accessible, not just in a phone that might be hard to reach if water is spreading near where you'd normally keep it charging. A few minutes of preparation now genuinely changes how the first ten minutes of a real emergency go.

What If You're Not Home When It Happens

If you have a smart water shutoff device or a leak sensor system, it can catch and respond to a burst pipe faster than a person discovering it after the fact. For a rental property, a vacation home, or any home that sits unoccupied for stretches of time, this kind of system is worth genuine consideration, since the damage from an unattended burst pipe compounds for however long it goes unnoticed.

The Same Advice Applies Across Buckeye, Goodyear, and Avondale

None of this is specific to one neighborhood or housing type. Whether you're in a newer Buckeye subdivision on a slab foundation or an established Avondale home with older plumbing, the first-ten-minutes sequence is the same. What differs is how likely a burst is to happen in the first place, and that's shaped by the specific age and condition of your home's plumbing, not by which city you're in.

If you take one thing from this, let it be finding your shutoff valve today. Everything else on this list matters, but that single piece of knowledge is what actually limits the damage in the moment it counts most.
