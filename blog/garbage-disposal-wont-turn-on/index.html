Garbage Disposal Won't Turn On? Here's What's Actually Happening

By the Buckeye 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-10

A garbage disposal that won't turn on at all feels like it must mean the motor has died, but that's actually one of the less common explanations. Most of these calls turn out to be something far simpler to fix.

Check The Reset Button First

Almost every disposal has a small red reset button on the bottom of the unit that trips when the motor overheats or jams, cutting power as a safety measure. Before assuming anything else is wrong, turn off the wall switch, locate the reset button, and press it firmly. If the disposal was simply tripped rather than actually failed, this alone often solves it.

If It Hums But Doesn't Spin

A humming sound without the blades turning almost always means a jam, not a dead motor. With the power off at the wall switch, look for the small hex-shaped opening on the bottom of the unit and use a disposal wrench or an Allen key to manually rotate the flywheel and clear the obstruction. Never reach into the disposal itself with your hand to do this, regardless of how confident you are the power is off.

Check The Circuit Breaker

If there's no sound at all, not even a hum, check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker specifically feeding the disposal circuit. This is a common and easy-to-overlook cause, especially if the disposal shares a circuit with a dishwasher or another kitchen appliance that recently drew a heavy load.

When It's Genuinely The Motor

If the reset button doesn't help, there's no jam to clear, and the breaker is fine, the motor itself may have failed. This is more likely on an older unit that's been running for many years, especially combined with Buckeye's hard-water scale accumulating around the flywheel and adding strain over time. At that point, replacement is usually the more practical option than attempting a motor repair.

Preventing This From Happening Again

Avoid putting fibrous vegetable scraps, grease, or starchy foods like rice and pasta down the disposal, since these are the most common causes of jams. Running cold water during and briefly after use helps flush waste through rather than letting it settle and contribute to future clogs.

Why Buckeye's Water Makes This Worse Over Time

Hard-water mineral deposits gradually coat the flywheel and internal components, adding resistance that makes the motor work harder for the same task. That extra strain compounds with normal wear, which is part of why a disposal here can start showing problems somewhat earlier than the same unit would in a city with softer water.

How To Tell If It's Really Time For Replacement

If the reset button, manual flywheel clearing, and breaker check all come up empty, and the unit is more than 8 to 10 years old, replacement is usually the more practical answer than continuing to troubleshoot a motor that's genuinely at the end of its life. A newer unit will also typically run more efficiently than an aging one that's been fighting mineral buildup for years.

What To Do In The Meantime

If your disposal is out of commission and you're waiting on a repair or replacement, avoid putting anything down that drain beyond normal dishwater, and use the sink's other basin if you have a double sink, to avoid a manual backup while the unit is offline.

Is It Ever Just A Bad Outlet?

If the disposal is on a switched outlet under the sink rather than hardwired, it's worth checking that the outlet itself is working, by plugging in another small appliance, before assuming the disposal or the wall switch is at fault. This is an easy step to overlook and occasionally the actual answer.

Choosing A Replacement

If replacement is the answer, horsepower and household size are the main sizing factors: a smaller household with light use can run a standard-duty unit comfortably, while a larger household or one that processes more food waste benefits from a higher-horsepower model. We'll help match the replacement to your actual usage rather than defaulting to whichever unit happens to be on the truck.

Installation Timing And What To Expect

A disposal replacement, once the correct unit is on hand, is typically completed in under an hour, including removing the old unit and connecting the new one to the existing plumbing and electrical. We test the new unit before considering the job finished, confirming it runs smoothly and drains properly under normal use.

One Last Thing Worth Checking

If the disposal seems fine but the sink itself is draining slowly, that's a separate issue from the disposal not turning on, and it's worth mentioning both symptoms when you call rather than assuming they're unrelated. Sometimes what looks like a disposal problem is actually a drain clog downstream that's making the disposal seem like it's malfunctioning when the disposal itself is working exactly as it should.

The Bottom Line

Most dead-seeming disposals aren't actually dead. Working through the reset button, a manual flywheel check, and the breaker panel in that order resolves the majority of these calls without ever needing a technician. When it genuinely is the motor, replacement is a quick, same-day fix rather than a drawn-out repair, and either way, you'll have a working disposal again the same day you call, without a lengthy wait for parts or scheduling delays.

When To Just Call Instead Of Troubleshooting

If you're not comfortable reaching under the sink to check the reset button or use the wrench opening, or if the unit shows any signs of a leak in addition to not turning on, it's reasonable to skip the troubleshooting steps and just call. There's no downside to having a technician handle the whole thing from the start if that's more comfortable than working through it yourself.
