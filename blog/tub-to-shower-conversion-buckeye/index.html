Renovating a Bathroom in Buckeye? What to Know Before Converting Your Tub to a Shower

By the Buckeye 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026-09-10

A tub-to-shower conversion is one of the more common bathroom renovation requests we get, often driven by accessibility needs, a desire for a more modern look, or simply not using the tub anymore. It's a bigger project than a like-for-like fixture swap, and it's worth understanding what's actually involved before committing.

Why People Convert

The most common reason we hear is accessibility, particularly from households in active-adult communities like PebbleCreek in Goodyear, where a step-in shower is simply safer and more practical long-term than climbing over a tub wall. Others convert for space efficiency in a smaller bathroom, or because the tub has gone unused for years and a shower better fits how the household actually lives.

The Plumbing Side Of The Conversion

Converting from a tub to a shower usually requires repositioning the drain, since a shower pan's drain location often differs from a tub's, and confirming the supply line and valve setup work with the new shower fixture rather than the old tub-and-shower combo. This is the part of the project that goes wrong most often when it's not handled by someone who does plumbing specifically, since a drain positioned even slightly off can cause standing water or slow drainage in the finished shower.

What Gets Discovered During Removal

Removing an old tub often reveals what's actually going on behind and beneath it: water damage from years of small leaks around the caulk line, deteriorated subfloor, or plumbing that needs attention beyond the fixture itself. We flag anything we find honestly before proceeding, since it changes the scope of the project and it's your decision how to handle it.

How This Fits With The Rest Of A Remodel

If you're doing a full bathroom remodel with new tile, fixtures, and a vanity alongside the conversion, the plumbing work generally happens early in the project sequence, before tile and surround work covers the areas that need to be accessible. Coordinating timing with whoever's handling the broader remodel avoids the plumbing work becoming the bottleneck that delays everything else.

Is It Worth It For Resale?

This depends on your home and your market. A conversion in a home with multiple other bathrooms that still have tubs is a lower-risk decision than converting the only tub in a home, since some buyers, particularly families with young children, specifically look for at least one tub. If resale is a real consideration, it's worth thinking through alongside the accessibility or lifestyle reasons driving the decision.

Fixture And Finish Choices

Once the plumbing decisions are settled, fixture choice mostly comes down to preference and budget: a fixed showerhead versus a handheld, a built-in bench or seat for accessibility, and the surround material, whether that's a one-piece acrylic unit or full tile. We handle the plumbing connections for any of these choices, but the finish decisions themselves are yours to make based on the overall bathroom design.

How Long The Plumbing Portion Takes

The plumbing work itself, removal, drain repositioning, and rough-in for the new fixture, is typically a one to two day portion of a larger project timeline, depending on complexity. The full conversion, including tile, surround installation, and finishing work, usually takes longer and is coordinated with whoever's handling that portion of the remodel.

What To Ask Before Committing

Before starting, it's worth asking specifically whether your home's existing drain and supply line positions will work with the shower configuration you want, or whether they'll need to be relocated. That answer affects both cost and timeline, and getting it upfront avoids a mid-project surprise once the old tub is already out.

Permits And Inspections

Depending on your municipality and the scope of the conversion, plumbing changes like drain relocation may require a permit and inspection. We handle the plumbing work to code regardless, and can advise on whether your specific project needs a permit pulled before work begins, which varies by jurisdiction across Buckeye, Goodyear, and Avondale.

Living Without A Second Bathroom During The Project

If the bathroom being converted is your only one, it's worth planning around the days the plumbing is disconnected. We coordinate scheduling to minimize how long water is off to that bathroom specifically, but a single-bathroom household should plan for at least a partial day without full functionality during the core plumbing work.

What Actually Determines The Final Cost

Beyond the fixture and finish choices, the biggest cost factor is usually how much the drain and supply lines need to move to accommodate the new shower configuration. A conversion that can reuse the existing drain location costs meaningfully less than one requiring the drain relocated to a different spot on the floor, since that work involves cutting into and rerouting plumbing under the slab or floor.

Getting An Accurate Quote Upfront

A quote that's confirmed after we've seen the actual plumbing behind your existing tub is far more reliable than a phone estimate based on a general description. We'll walk through the specific configuration on-site and give you a real number before any work begins, so there's no gap between what was discussed and what the project actually costs.

Making The Final Decision

Whether the driving reason is accessibility, style, or simply not using the tub, the plumbing question is the same: what will it take to make the conversion work well in your specific bathroom. Getting that answer from an on-site assessment, rather than a generic estimate, is what actually lets you decide with confidence, and it's a conversation worth having even before you've fully committed to the project.
