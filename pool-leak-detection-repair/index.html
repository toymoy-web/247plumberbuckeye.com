Overview

Pool Leak Detection & Repair In Buckeye, AZ

Buckeye's newer subdivisions, including Verrado, Tartesso, and Sundance, have a real, substantial share of homes with backyard pools, and pool leaks are a genuinely common call here given the desert heat and heavy pool use through most of the year.

A pool that's losing more water than normal evaporation explains, roughly a quarter inch to half inch a day depending on weather, usually has a real leak somewhere in the plumbing lines, the equipment pad, or occasionally the shell itself. We check the most likely and least invasive sources first.

Local Causes

Why This Happens In Buckeye

Underground Plumbing Line Leaks

The suction and return lines running underground between the pool and equipment pad can develop leaks at pipe joints, especially as ground conditions shift with seasonal moisture changes.

Equipment Pad Leaks

Pump seals, filter O-rings, and heater connections are common, relatively easy-to-fix leak points that don't require excavation.

Skimmer and Light Niche Leaks

The skimmer throat and light niche fitting are known leak points where the pool shell meets built-in hardware, particularly as sealants age.

Signs To Watch For

Signs You Need This Service

  • ✓
    Water loss noticeably faster than the bucket test suggests for evaporation alone
  • ✓
    Wet spots or unusually green grass near underground plumbing runs
  • ✓
    Air bubbles in the pool's return jets
  • ✓
    A consistently running pump trying to compensate for lost pressure
  • ✓
    Cracking or settling in decking near the pool's edge
Our Process

How We Find The Actual Leak

We start with a bucket test to confirm whether water loss exceeds normal evaporation, then check the equipment pad for visible leaks before moving to electronic leak detection on the underground plumbing lines if needed. Most equipment-pad leaks are visually confirmable and don't require specialized detection equipment at all.

Just Topping Off The Water Every Week? Read This First
A lot of pool owners assume heavy water loss in Arizona summers is just evaporation and keep topping it off. If you're adding water more than once or twice a week during peak summer heat, that's usually past what evaporation alone explains, and it's worth an actual check rather than continuing to top it off indefinitely.

Decision Guide

Just Topping Off Water vs. Proper Leak Detection & Repair

Just Topping Off Water

  • Doesn't address the underlying leak, so the water loss continues indefinitely
  • Can mask a growing leak until it causes visible yard or structural damage
  • Wastes water and money every week the leak goes unaddressed

Proper Leak Detection & Repair

  • Confirms whether water loss is a real leak or normal evaporation
  • Locates the specific leak point before any repair is quoted
  • Stops ongoing water loss and the risk of secondary yard damage
